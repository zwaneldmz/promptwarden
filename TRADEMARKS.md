# Trademark Policy

"PromptWarden" and the PromptWarden logo (once one exists) are trademarks of
the PromptWarden project maintainers. This policy is separate from the
Apache-2.0 license that covers the code: you get broad rights to the
software, and narrow rights to the name.

## What the code license already gives you

Apache-2.0 lets you use, modify, and redistribute this code — including
commercially — without asking permission. That includes running your own
fork in production. See LICENSE and NOTICE.

## What the code license does not give you

Apache-2.0 Section 6 explicitly withholds trademark rights. So, without
separate written permission from the maintainers, you may not:

- Distribute a modified build, fork, or derivative work under the name
  "PromptWarden," a confusingly similar name, or the project logo.
- Use "PromptWarden" in the name of a competing product, service, MSP
  offering, or company.
- Use the PromptWarden name or logo in a way that states or implies your
  fork, service, or organization is officially affiliated with, endorsed
  by, or produced by the PromptWarden project or its maintainers, when it
  is not.
- Register "PromptWarden" or a confusingly similar term as a trademark,
  domain name, app-store listing name, or social handle.

## What is always fine

- Truthful, non-misleading references to the project by name — "built on
  PromptWarden," "compatible with PromptWarden policy schema," "based on
  PromptWarden v0.x" — in documentation, blog posts, or comparison pages.
- Internal forks that are never distributed or offered as a service to
  others.
- Bug reports, patches, and contributions submitted back to the project.

## Forks

If you distribute a modified version of this software to others — as a
package, a hosted service, or a competing product — rename it. Pick a name
that does not include "PromptWarden" and is not confusingly similar. You
may keep truthful attribution ("a fork of PromptWarden") in your
documentation.
